Tucked into the Rif Mountains in northern Morocco, Chefchaouen is famous for one thing above everything else: almost every wall, staircase, and doorway in its old medina is painted in shades of blue. Photos of it circulate constantly online, and the town lives up to them in person - it's one of the most genuinely photogenic places in the country, and quieter and slower-paced than Marrakech or Fes.

This guide covers how to get there, when to go, what to actually do once you arrive, and the practical details that make a visit run smoothly.

## Getting to Chefchaouen

Chefchaouen doesn't have its own airport, so you'll arrive overland from one of the nearby cities:

- **From Tangier:** About 2.5 hours by grand taxi or CTM/Supratours bus - the easiest and most common route for travelers arriving by ferry from Spain.
- **From Fes:** Around 4 hours by bus or grand taxi, sometimes with a change in Ouazzane.
- **From Casablanca or Rabat:** 5-6 hours by bus. Most visitors coming from further south combine Chefchaouen with a stop in Fes or Tangier rather than a direct trip.

Grand taxis (shared, or reserved as a private "taxi special") are faster and more flexible than the bus, but cost more. CTM is the most reliable bus operator if you'd rather book a fixed seat in advance.

## Best Time to Visit Chefchaouen

- **Spring (March-May) and Autumn (September-November):** The best windows. Mild temperatures for walking the steep medina streets and hiking outside town, without summer's heat.
- **Summer (June-August):** Warm but more bearable than Marrakech or Fes thanks to the mountain elevation - still, expect it to be busy with both Moroccan and international tourists.
- **Winter (December-February):** Cool and sometimes rainy, with occasional snow on the surrounding peaks. The medina is quieter, and the blue walls against grey winter skies have their own appeal, but pack for cold evenings.

## Where to Stay

Nearly everyone stays inside or right at the edge of the medina, and it's worth doing - Chefchaouen is small enough that a centrally located guesthouse puts you a few minutes' walk from everything. Rooftop terraces are the norm even in budget guesthouses, and many face directly onto the Rif Mountains, which makes sunset from your own riad one of the better free things to do here.

Book ahead in spring and summer weekends, when day-trippers from Tangier and Fes fill up the town's limited number of rooms.

## Top Things to Do in Chefchaouen

**Wander the medina** - The main activity, and arguably the whole point of visiting. There's no must-see checklist here; the best use of your time is simply getting lost in the blue alleyways, especially in the early morning before the day-trip crowds arrive and the light is softest for photos.

**Plaza Uta el-Hammam** - The main square, lined with cafes and anchored by the Kasbah and the Grand Mosque. A natural place to start and end each day.

**The Kasbah Museum** - A restored 15th-century fortress right on the main square, with a small ethnographic museum and a garden. The tower has one of the better rooftop views over the town.

**The Spanish Mosque** - A short, moderately steep hike (about 30-45 minutes each way) up the hillside east of the medina to an unused mosque, mainly visited for the sunset view over the entire Blue City from above. Bring water and decent shoes.

**Ras el-Maa waterfall** - A small waterfall right at the edge of the medina where local women still wash wool and clothes by hand. Easy to reach on foot and a good short walk if you don't have time for the Spanish Mosque hike.

**Shopping** - Chefchaouen is known for wool products (blankets, ponchos, garments) and local goat cheese, and the tourist pressure here is noticeably lighter than in Marrakech's souks, which makes for a more relaxed bargaining experience.

## What to Eat

Chefchaouen's food scene is simpler than Marrakech or Fes, but a few things are worth seeking out:

- **Goat cheese** - produced locally in the surrounding hills and sold fresh in the medina.
- **Bissara** - a thick fava bean soup, a northern Moroccan breakfast staple, especially in the cooler months.
- **Tagine and couscous** - the same staples found across Morocco, generally simpler and less spiced here than further south.
- **Mint tea** - as everywhere in Morocco, offered constantly and worth slowing down for rather than rushing through.

## Practical Tips

- **Photography and etiquette:** Many residents are used to tourists photographing their doorways and walls, but always ask before photographing people directly, and expect some vendors near popular photo spots to ask for a small tip.
- **Walking shoes:** The medina is built on a hillside with a lot of stairs and uneven, sometimes slippery, painted stone. Flat, sturdy shoes make a real difference.
- **Day trip vs. overnight:** Chefchaouen is doable as a long day trip from Tangier, but an overnight stay lets you see the town in the early morning and evening light, when it's at its quietest and most photogenic - we'd recommend at least one night if your schedule allows it.
- **Cash:** Smaller than Marrakech or Fes in terms of card acceptance - carry cash (Moroccan dirham) for guesthouses, cafes, and the souk.
- **Hash offers:** The Rif region is known for cannabis cultivation, and it's common to be approached with offers in and around the medina. It's illegal in Morocco; a polite decline is enough.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is one day enough for Chefchaouen?**
You can see the main square, the Kasbah, and walk a good portion of the medina in a single day. Staying one night lets you catch sunrise or sunset without rushing, which is when the town looks its best.

**How do I get from Chefchaouen to Fes or Tangier?**
Both CTM buses and grand taxis run regularly between Chefchaouen and each city. Book the earlier buses in high season, as afternoon departures sell out first.

**Is Chefchaouen safe for solo travelers?**
Yes - it's one of the more relaxed and low-pressure towns in Morocco for solo travelers, including women traveling alone, though the usual common-sense precautions (well-lit paths at night, agreeing on taxi fares in advance) still apply.

Chefchaouen rewards slowing down more than almost anywhere else in Morocco. Give it at least one full day, get up early enough to see the medina before the crowds, and leave room in your itinerary to simply wander.
