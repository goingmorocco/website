Ask someone to picture Morocco and you'll get camels, medinas, and maybe a rug or two. Almost nobody pictures a beach, which is a genuinely strange oversight for a country with over 3,000 kilometers of coastline running along both the Atlantic and the Mediterranean. The desert and the souks get all the attention while an entire coastline of surf towns, resort strips, and dramatic rock arches sits there quietly being spectacular.

Here's where to actually go, organized by what kind of beach day you're after.

## Best for Surfing

**Taghazout and Imsouane** are the two names that matter here, and we've written full guides to both: [Taghazout](/post/taghazout-morocco/) is the bigger, more developed surf town with a real scene built around it, while [Imsouane](/post/imsouane-morocco-the-hidden-surf-paradise-guide/) is quieter and home to one of the longest, gentlest right-hand waves in the world - genuinely one of the best beginner-friendly breaks anywhere. If surfing itself is the goal rather than just the beach, our [full surfing guide](/post/surfing-in-morocco-best-spots-and-what-to-know/) covers spots, seasons, and what to pack in more detail.

## Best for a Classic Resort Beach Day

**Agadir** is Morocco's answer to a straightforward, sun-loungers-and-a-cocktail beach holiday - a long, wide, ten-kilometer stretch of sand backed by a proper resort strip. It's more polished and less "traditionally Moroccan" than the rest of the country, which is exactly the point for a lot of visitors. Our [honest take on whether Agadir is worth visiting](/post/agadir-is-booming-with-tourists/) covers the trade-offs, and if you're looking for somewhere to actually stay, we've reviewed both the [Sofitel Agadir Royal Bay Resort](/post/sofitel-agadir-royal-bay-resort-complete-guide-review/) and the [Caribbean Village Agador](/post/caribbean-village-agador-in-agadir-morocco-hotel-review-guide/).

## Best for Dramatic Scenery (and Fewer Tourists)

**Legzira Beach**, near Sidi Ifni in the south, is home to massive red sandstone arches carved by the Atlantic over centuries - one of the most striking natural sights on the entire coastline, and one that most visitors never even hear about. **Asilah**, a small, whitewashed coastal town about 45km south of Tangier, offers Atlantic views from its old ramparts along with a genuinely relaxed, artsy atmosphere. We cover both in our [hidden gems guide](/post/hidden-gems-in-morocco-most-tourists-miss/), and honestly, if "empty beach with dramatic scenery and no crowd" sounds better to you than "resort strip," start there instead of Agadir.

## Best for Windsurfing and Kitesurfing

**Essaouira** earns its "Wind City of Africa" nickname honestly, and its beach is built for exactly this - see our [Essaouira guide](/post/essaouira-travel-guide-what-to-know-before-you-visit/) for the rest of what to do in town. Further south, **Dakhla** has developed into one of the world's best year-round windsurfing and kitesurfing destinations, thanks to a lagoon that stays flat and windy pretty much on demand - we mention it alongside other adventure-sport regions in our [guide to Morocco's top outdoor activity spots](/post/spots-in-morocco-for-outdoor-activities/).

## Atlantic vs. Mediterranean Coast

Most of what gets talked about (Agadir, Essaouira, Taghazout, Imsouane, Legzira) sits on the Atlantic side, which tends to mean cooler water, bigger swells, and a more rugged feel. The Mediterranean side, up around Tangier, has its own calmer, warmer stretches of sand - our [guide to whether Tangier is worth visiting](/post/is-tangier-worth-visiting-let-s-break-it-down/) touches on its beaches if that end of the country is on your route.

## Practical Tips

- **Beach culture varies by location.** Resort beaches in Agadir and tourist-oriented spots are generally relaxed about swimwear; smaller local public beaches lean more conservative, so it's worth reading the room (or just looking around) before assuming anything goes.
- **Bring a wetsuit or at least a rash guard for the Atlantic.** It looks warm. It is frequently not warm. Ask any surfer who's learned this the hard way.
- **Spring and autumn hit the sweet spot** for most of the coast - warm enough to enjoy the beach without the peak summer crowds (or the peak summer heat, if you're also planning inland stops).
- **Sun protection matters more than you'd think**, even on breezy days - the coastal wind has a well-earned reputation for hiding how much sun you're actually getting.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What's the best Moroccan beach for a family holiday?**
Agadir is the easiest choice - long sandy beach, resort infrastructure, and calmer water than the more exposed surf towns further north.

**Are Moroccan beaches good for swimming, or mostly for surfing?**
Both - Agadir and the Mediterranean coast around Tangier are generally calmer and better suited to swimming, while the Atlantic surf towns have more current and swell.

**Is it worth visiting a beach town if I'm not into surfing or watersports?**
Absolutely - Legzira's rock arches and Asilah's old town are worth the trip on scenery and atmosphere alone, no board required.

Morocco's coastline does not get nearly enough credit, and that's honestly good news for you - less crowded, still stunning, and only a short detour from whatever else is already on your itinerary.
