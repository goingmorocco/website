A week is the sweet spot for a first Morocco trip that wants it all - not just one city, not just the desert, but the classic combination: the imperial cities that give the country its historical weight, plus a genuine night in the Sahara rather than a rushed day-trip version of it. This itinerary threads Marrakech, Fes, and Chefchaouen together with a real detour south to Merzouga, so nothing on the list feels like an afterthought.

## The Itinerary

**Day 1: Start in Marrakech**
Settle in and explore the city's highlights - Jemaa el-Fnaa, the souks, the Bahia Palace, and whatever else made your list. Our [Marrakech travel guide](/post/marrakech-travel-guide-what-to-know-before-you-visit/) covers the full rundown if you want to plan this day properly.

**Day 2: Into the Atlas and Toward the Desert**
Cross the High Atlas via the Tizi n'Tichka pass, stopping at the UNESCO-listed Ait Ben Haddou kasbah and the old Atlas Film Studios in Ouarzazate, before continuing to the Dades Valley for the night.

**Day 3: Gorges and the Sahara**
Wind through the Dades and Todra Gorges, then push on to Merzouga in time for a sunset camel trek into the Erg Chebbi dunes and a night in a desert camp under genuinely dark skies. Our [Merzouga guide](/post/merzouga-travel-guide-what-to-know-before-you-visit/) covers exactly what this night involves.

**Day 4: Sunrise in the Dunes, North to Fes**
An early camel ride to catch sunrise over the Sahara, then a long but scenic drive north through the Ziz Valley and the Middle Atlas cedar forests, arriving in Fes by evening.

**Day 5: Fes, the Cultural Capital**
A full day in Fes el Bali - the tanneries, the Bou Inania Madrasa, and the sheer experience of getting theatrically lost in the world's largest car-free urban area. Our [Fes travel guide](/post/fes-travel-guide-what-to-know-before-you-visit/) covers this in more (occasionally sarcastic) detail.

**Day 6: North to Chefchaouen**
A shorter travel day into the Rif Mountains, ending in the Blue City for an afternoon and evening of wandering blue-washed alleyways. Our [Chefchaouen guide](/post/chefchaouen-blue-city-travel-guide-what-to-know-before-you-visit/) has the rundown on what to prioritize with limited time there.

**Day 7: Onward Travel**
Depending on the operator, the trip wraps up with a drop-off in Fes, Tangier, Rabat, or Casablanca for onward travel or your flight home.

## What's Typically Included

- Private, air-conditioned transport for the full week
- A driver/guide throughout
- Camel trek and one night in a Merzouga desert camp
- Hotel or riad accommodation for the other nights
- Most breakfasts and several dinners

Lunches, individual site entrance fees, and tips are usually separate - confirm the exact breakdown with the operator before booking.

## Who This Trip Is For

This is the itinerary for a first-timer who wants to leave Morocco feeling like they saw the country, not just one corner of it - history, mountains, a real desert night, and enough variety that no two days feel the same. It's a lot of driving by design, so pace yourself and enjoy the scenery along the way rather than treating the transit days as dead time.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is a week enough to properly see imperial cities and the desert?**
It's tight but workable - this itinerary is built specifically to balance both without feeling rushed, though each individual city gets less time than a dedicated city-only trip would allow.

**Does it matter which direction I do the loop in?**
Not really - both directions hit the same stops. Pick whichever matches your flight in and out (Marrakech vs. Tangier/Casablanca) to avoid extra backtracking.

**Can this be extended if I have more time?**
Yes - adding an extra day or two in Fes or Marrakech, or a stop in Essaouira on the way back, are common and easy additions if your schedule allows.

Seven days, four unmistakably different landscapes, and one very long but very rewarding loop - if you want the "greatest hits" version of Morocco without cutting corners on the desert, this is the itinerary that gets you there.
