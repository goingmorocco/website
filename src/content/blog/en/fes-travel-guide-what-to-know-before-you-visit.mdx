Let's get this out of the way immediately: you are going to get lost in Fes. Not "might." Will. The Fes el Bali medina has somewhere around 9,000 alleyways, none of which believe in right angles, and your phone's GPS will simply give up and stare blankly at a little blue dot that isn't moving because it genuinely cannot figure out which floor of which building you're currently under. This is, weirdly, part of the charm. Fes is the oldest and best-preserved of Morocco's imperial cities, and it rewards exactly the kind of aimless wandering that would stress you out anywhere else.

Here's everything you need to know so your "getting lost" is a fun story and not a minor crisis.

## Getting to Fes

Fes has its own airport (Fes-Saiss, code FEZ), with a decent number of connections from Europe, though fewer than Marrakech or Casablanca. If you're coming from elsewhere in Morocco, the train is genuinely one of the best parts of the trip - ONCF trains connect Fes to Casablanca, Rabat, Meknes, and Tangier comfortably, and honestly beat flying for anything under about 5 hours.

From the Fes train station or airport, a petit taxi into the medina area is cheap and easy. Just know that no taxi can actually drive into the old medina itself (it's pedestrian-only, mules included), so you'll be dropped at one of the main gates - Bab Boujloud is the usual one - and someone from your riad will likely meet you there, because otherwise, see: getting lost, above.

## Best Time to Visit Fes

- **Spring (March-May) and Autumn (September-November):** The sweet spot. Comfortable for hours of medina walking without melting.
- **Summer (June-August):** Hot, and Fes sits inland so it doesn't get the coastal relief Essaouira or Casablanca do. Expect it to regularly hit 35&deg;C+ in July and August. Doable, just plan your big walking days for the morning.
- **Winter (December-February):** Mild by day, properly cold at night. Riads with open courtyards look beautiful and feel like a walk-in fridge after dark, so pack layers and don't be shy about asking for extra blankets.

## Where to Stay

Stay inside the medina. Not "near" it - inside it. Yes, it means dragging your suitcase over uneven stone (or, more likely, having a guesthouse porter do it for you), but waking up in a converted 300-year-old riad with a plunge pool and a rooftop breakfast is genuinely one of the best hotel experiences in the country, and staying outside the medina turns Fes into a series of taxi rides instead of a place you actually live in for a few days.

## Top Things to Do in Fes

**Just wander Fes el Bali** - Again: this is the actual point. The medina is the largest car-free urban area in the world, and it's basically a living medieval city rather than a preserved-for-tourists version of one. Give yourself at least one morning with no plan whatsoever.

**Al-Qarawiyyin Mosque and University** - Founded in 859 AD and considered by some historians to be the oldest continuously operating university in the world. Non-Muslims can't enter, but you can peek through the doors, and the surrounding streets are some of the most atmospheric in the whole medina.

**Bou Inania Madrasa** - A 14th-century Islamic school with some of the most jaw-dropping tilework, carved cedar, and stucco you'll see anywhere in Morocco, and unlike the Qarawiyyin, you can actually go inside. Non-negotiable stop.

**The Tanneries** - Yes, the famous ones from every Instagram photo of Fes. You'll be handed a sprig of mint on arrival, and you'll understand why about four seconds later - the tanning process still uses pigeon droppings and cow urine, and it smells exactly as advertised. Go anyway. Watching leather get dyed by hand in stone vats the same way it's been done for centuries is worth the smell, and it's genuinely one of the most unique sights in the country.

**Fes el Jdid and the Royal Palace gates** - The "newer" part of the old city (built a mere 700 years ago), home to the seven golden doors of the Royal Palace, which you can't enter but absolutely can photograph.

**Merenid Tombs at sunset** - A ruined hilltop tomb complex just outside the medina walls, and the single best spot to watch the sun go down over the entire city. Bring a bottle of water, grab a taxi up (it's a proper climb otherwise), and just sit for a while.

## What to Eat in Fes

Fes is considered by a lot of Moroccans to be the country's culinary capital, so eat accordingly:

- **Pastilla** - Fes is widely credited as its birthplace, traditionally a sweet-savory pigeon pie dusted with cinnamon and sugar. If you only try it once in Morocco, try it here.
- **Harira** - a hearty tomato, lentil, and chickpea soup, and a genuinely great cold-weather lunch after a morning of medina walking.
- **Fes-style tagines** - typically a bit more refined and less rustic than what you'll find further south, often built around preserved lemon and olives.
- **Mint tea** - obviously. If you want the backstory on why it's poured from a height and offered to literally everyone, we've got a whole [guide to Moroccan mint tea](/post/discovering-the-heart-of-moroccan-hospitality-mint-tea/).

## Practical Tips (aka Things I Wish Someone Had Told Me)

- **Hire a guide for your first medina walk, or let your riad arrange one.** Not because you need a bodyguard, but because a good guide turns "I am lost and slightly panicking" into "wow, look at this 600-year-old fountain I would have walked straight past." Worth every dirham.
- **Ignore the "helpful strangers."** Random guys who insist the tanneries/madrasa/whatever "is closed today, but I know another way" are, without exception, trying to walk you to a shop that pays them a commission. A polite "no thank you" and continuing to walk works every time.
- **Wear shoes you don't mind getting dirty.** Between the tanneries, the stone alleys, and the occasional donkey traffic jam, the medina is not the place for your nice white sneakers.
- **Bargain, but don't stress about it.** You will occasionally overpay by a few dollars for something. This is fine. Nobody's vacation was ruined by a $3 markup on a leather bag.
- **Fridays are quieter.** It's the main prayer day, and some shops close for a few hours around midday - plan indoor activities like the madrasa or a museum for the morning if you're visiting on a Friday.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How many days do I need in Fes?**
Two full days covers the highlights comfortably; three lets you actually enjoy getting lost instead of just tolerating it.

**Is the medina safe?**
Yes, remarkably so, even in its most maze-like corners. The "danger" in Fes is 90% about wasting time or money to pushy touts, not personal safety.

**Do I really need a guide?**
Need, no. Want, probably yes - at least for your first walk through Fes el Bali. After that, you'll have your bearings (mostly) and can wander solo.

Fes isn't a city you "do" in an afternoon between photo stops - it's one you get slightly overwhelmed by, then slowly fall for. Budget the time, embrace the getting-lost part, and bring an appetite.
