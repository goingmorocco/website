Say "Morocco" and most people picture camels and medinas, not a wetsuit. Which is exactly why the Atlantic coast here has stayed relatively under-the-radar as a surf destination, despite offering some of the most consistent, world-class point breaks in Africa. European surfers have quietly known about this for decades. It's time everyone else caught up.

Here's where to go, when to go, and why "sunny Morocco" doesn't mean you can skip the wetsuit.

## Best Surf Spots in Morocco

**Taghazout** - The undisputed capital of Moroccan surfing, and the reason the whole area north of Agadir has turned into a proper surf town over the past couple of decades. Home to famous breaks like Anchor Point (a long, powerful right-hander for more experienced surfers) alongside a handful of mellower, beginner-friendly spots nearby. It's also just a genuinely fun place to hang around between sessions - our [full Taghazout guide](/post/taghazout-morocco/) covers the food, the vibe, and what else to do when you're not in the water.

**Imsouane** - About an hour up the coast from Taghazout, and home to "the Bay," one of the longest right-hand point breaks in the world - a wave so mellow and long that it's become a rite of passage for longboarders and improving beginners alike. It's sleepier and less developed than Taghazout, which is exactly the appeal. We go deep on it in our [Imsouane surf guide](/post/imsouane-morocco-the-hidden-surf-paradise-guide/).

**Essaouira** - Better known for windsurfing and kitesurfing thanks to its relentless wind (we cover that side of things in our [Essaouira travel guide](/post/essaouira-travel-guide-what-to-know-before-you-visit/)), but there's still surfable swell here for anyone splitting their trip between wave-riding and wind-riding.

**Agadir** - Less of a surf destination in its own right, more of a comfortable base with resort-style accommodation if you want an easy launch point for day trips up to Taghazout. If that's your plan, our review of the [Sofitel Agadir Royal Bay Resort](/post/sofitel-agadir-royal-bay-resort-complete-guide-review/) is worth a look for the "come back to a nice hotel after surfing all day" crowd.

## Best Time to Surf in Morocco

- **Autumn and winter (October-March):** The biggest, most consistent swells hit the coast, drawing more experienced surfers chasing real size and power. Water and air temperatures both drop, so a proper wetsuit isn't optional.
- **Spring and summer (April-September):** Smaller, more manageable waves - genuinely the better window for beginners and anyone who'd rather not get worked by a winter swell on day one. Warmer water too, though a spring suit or shortie is still usually worth packing.

## Do You Need Experience?

Not at all, especially in Imsouane, where the wave's length and gentleness make it one of the most forgiving beginner and improver spots anywhere in the world - you'll have time to actually think about your footwork instead of just surviving the wave. Taghazout has more of a range, from beginner beaches to genuinely serious breaks like Anchor Point that are better left to confident, experienced surfers. Surf schools and camps are widely available in both towns if you want structured lessons rather than winging it.

## What to Pack

- **A wetsuit, regardless of season.** The Atlantic here is cooler than the desert heat inland would suggest - a 3/2mm suit covers most of the year, with a thicker one worth considering in winter.
- **Reef booties if you're heading to rockier breaks** - ask locally, since not every spot needs them.
- **Sunscreen made for water sports.** You're out longer than you think, and the glare off the water is no joke.
- **A soft-top or rental board if you're a beginner**, rather than bringing your own - most surf towns have well-stocked rental shops, so there's no need to fly with a board bag.

## Practical Tips

- **Book a surf camp or guesthouse in advance during peak season** (autumn/winter for the swell chasers, summer for the beginner crowd) - both Taghazout and Imsouane fill up.
- **Respect the lineup.** Both towns get busy with international surfers and locals alike; the usual surf etiquette (don't drop in, wait your turn) applies just as much here as anywhere else.
- **Combine it with the rest of the coast.** Taghazout and Imsouane pair naturally with a stop in Essaouira if you want variety beyond straight wave-riding.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need to be an experienced surfer to visit Taghazout or Imsouane?**
No - both areas have beginner-friendly spots and surf schools. Imsouane in particular is one of the most forgiving beginner waves anywhere.

**Is Morocco surfing only for advanced surfers chasing big winter swells?**
Not at all - summer brings smaller, gentler waves that are genuinely a better fit for beginners than the bigger winter swells.

**Do I need to bring my own surfboard?**
No, rental shops and surf schools in both Taghazout and Imsouane are well stocked - traveling with your own board is only worth it if you're particular about your setup.

Morocco's surf scene isn't a secret anymore, but it's still nowhere near as crowded as the coasts everyone already knows about. Pack the wetsuit, pick your wave length preference, and go find out why surfers have been quietly coming back here for years.
