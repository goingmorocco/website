## Getting to Marrakech

Marrakech Menara Airport (RAK) is the main gateway for most international visitors and sits only about 15 minutes from the city center, which makes it one of the more convenient major airports in North Africa. From the airport you have three realistic options: a metered petit taxi, a pre-booked private transfer, or your riad arranging a pickup for you.

## Where to Stay: Medina vs. Gueliz

This is the single biggest decision that shapes your trip.

**The Medina (old city)** is where you want to stay if you're after atmosphere â narrow alleyways, rooftop breakfasts, and a traditional riad experience just steps from Jemaa el-Fnaa. The trade-off is noise, and navigating on foot (cars can't enter most of it) takes some getting used to on day one.

**Gueliz (Ville Nouvelle)** is the modern, French-built part of the city â wider streets, more international restaurants, and easier for taxis and ride-hailing. It's a good fit if you want a quieter base and don't mind a short taxi ride into the old city each day.

- **Tagine** â slow-cooked stew, usually lamb, chicken, or vegetable, named after the conical clay pot it's cooked in.

## Practical Tips

- **Currency:** The Moroccan dirham (MAD). Cash is still king in the souks; cards work in most riads, restaurants, and malls.
- **Language:** Arabic and Amazigh (Berber) are official languages; French is widely spoken as a second language, and English is common in tourist-facing businesses.
- **Dress code:** Marrakech is relatively liberal by Moroccan standards, but modest dress (shoulders and knees covered) is appreciated, especially in the Medina and at religious sites.
- **Bargaining:** Expected in the souks, not expected in fixed-price shops, restaurants, or with metered taxis.
- **Safety:** Marrakech is generally safe for tourists, including solo travelers. The most common issue is overly persistent vendors or unofficial "guides" in the Medina â a polite but firm "no, thank you" is usually enough.
- **Getting around:** Petit taxis (small, metered, for up to 3 people) are the easiest way to move between the Medina and Gueliz. Agree on a price or ask for the meter before you get in.
