Quick fact to win an argument later: the animal you're about to ride is technically a dromedary, not a camel - one hump, not two, and the "camel" label is just what everyone uses anyway, so we will too. What nobody tells you in advance is that the scariest part of the whole experience isn't the height or the swaying, it's the exact moment the animal stands up. Camels rise back-legs-first, which means for about two seconds you're pitched forward at an angle that makes you seriously reconsider your life choices, before it straightens out and you're just... up there. Fine. Regal, even.

Here's everything else worth knowing before you climb on.

## Where to Ride a Camel in Morocco

**Merzouga / Erg Chebbi** - The classic, and honestly the one worth prioritizing if you only do this once. A sunset ride into the dunes, often paired with an overnight desert camp, is the single most iconic Sahara experience in the country. Our [Merzouga guide](/post/merzouga-travel-guide-what-to-know-before-you-visit/) covers the full desert camp experience if you're building this into a longer trip.

**Zagora / Erg Chigaga** - The quieter, more remote alternative to Merzouga, with the same sunset-ride-plus-camp formula but noticeably fewer other groups of tourists doing the exact same thing at the exact same dune. We compare the two directly in [Erg Chebbi vs. Erg Chigaga](/post/erg-chebbi-vs-erg-chigaga-which-is-better/).

**Agafay Desert, near Marrakech** - Not the Sahara (it's a rocky, moon-like desert, not sand dunes), but close enough to Marrakech for a half-day or sunset trip without committing to the full multi-day desert tour. A solid option if your schedule is tight but you still want the photo.

**Essaouira beach** - A completely different vibe: camel (and horse) rides along the Atlantic coastline rather than through dunes. Worth doing if you're already there and want a coastal version of the experience - see our [Essaouira guide](/post/essaouira-travel-guide-what-to-know-before-you-visit/) for the rest of what to do while you're in town.

## What It's Actually Like

Rides are typically slow, guided, single-file affairs, with a handler leading a string of several camels roped together rather than you steering your own. It's not fast, it's not bumpy in the way horseback riding can be, and it's genuinely more comfortable than it looks, aside from that initial standing-up lurch. A typical sunset ride into the dunes runs somewhere around 45 minutes to an hour each way - enough time to actually feel like an experience, not just a photo op, without leaving you saddle-sore for a week.

## Practical Tips

- **Wear looser, comfortable clothing.** You'll be seated for a while in a position you're probably not used to.
- **Sunglasses and a scarf or hat.** Between the sun and the occasional gust of sand, your face will thank you.
- **Mind your camera.** Phones and cameras have a well-documented history of leaping out of pockets during the standing-up-and-sitting-down moments. A wrist strap or zipped pocket saves a lot of regret.
- **Skip it if you have back or hip problems.** The gait is gentle overall, but the sitting position and that initial lurch aren't ideal if you're already dealing with joint or spine issues - ask your operator about alternatives if this is a concern.
- **Tipping is customary** for the handler, even though it's usually built into a tour package - a small additional tip is appreciated and not expected to be large.

## A Word on Animal Welfare

This is worth taking seriously rather than skipping past. Reputable operators rotate and rest their camels, keep them properly fed and watered, and don't overload them with back-to-back rides all day in peak heat. If you see camels that look visibly thin, exhausted, or are being pushed well beyond a reasonable group size and pace, that's a signal to choose a different operator next time (or say something, politely, to your guide). Booking through an established riad, tour company, or desert camp - rather than the cheapest random operator standing at the roadside - is generally the safer bet for both your experience and the animal's wellbeing.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is a camel ride worth it, or is it just a tourist trap?**
It's touristy, sure, but it's touristy for a reason - watching the Sahara's dunes change color from camelback at sunset is one of those experiences that genuinely lives up to the hype, unlike a fair number of "must-do" activities that don't.

**How long does a typical ride last?**
Most sunset or sunrise rides run 45 minutes to an hour each way. Shorter 15-20 minute rides are also common in places like Agafay or Essaouira for a quicker taste of the experience.

**Are camel rides safe for kids or older travelers?**
Generally yes - the pace is slow and the handler is in full control - but it's worth mentioning any mobility concerns to your operator beforehand so they can seat people appropriately and assist with mounting and dismounting.

However touristy it sounds on paper, a camel ride at sunset in the Sahara is one of those rare Morocco activities that photographs exactly as well as it feels in the moment. Just brace yourself for the stand-up.
