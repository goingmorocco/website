Three days is a tight window for Morocco, so this itinerary doesn't try to cram in the whole country - it does one thing well: Marrakech as your starting and ending point, and the High Atlas Mountains as the actual main event in between. If you want dunes and camel treks, that's a different trip. This one is for anyone who wants real mountain trekking, Berber villages, and some quiet away from the souks, without committing to a week.

## The Itinerary

**Day 1: Marrakech to the Mountains**
Depart Marrakech and drive to Imlil, the small mountain village that serves as the gateway to the High Atlas. From there, trek through the Aroumd valley and over the Tizi Mzik pass before reaching your first overnight stop, with a picnic lunch along the way.

**Day 2: Deeper Into the Atlas**
A full trekking day through the mountains, crossing additional passes and valleys, with a guide leading the way and lunch taken on the trail. You'll overnight in a second mountain village, well away from anything resembling a tourist strip.

**Day 3: Back to Marrakech**
A final trek back toward Imlil, crossing the Tizi Oussem area, before meeting your driver for the return trip to Marrakech.

## What's Typically Included

- Private, air-conditioned transport between Marrakech and Imlil
- A professional hiking guide for the full trek
- High Atlas Mountains entrance fees
- Breakfast, lunch, and dinner throughout
- Two nights of mountain accommodation (guesthouse-style, not a riad or hotel)

Maximum elevation on this route reaches roughly 2,400 meters (about 7,874 feet), so a reasonable level of fitness matters more here than on a typical sightseeing trip - this is genuine trekking, not a scenic drive with photo stops.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do I need trekking experience for this itinerary?**
No technical experience is required, but you should be reasonably fit and comfortable with multiple hours of walking on mountain trails each day.

**Will I still get time in Marrakech itself?**
This itinerary is built around the mountains rather than the city - plan for extra days in Marrakech before or after if you want proper time in the souks and medina too.

**What's the accommodation like in the mountains?**
Simple, guesthouse-style lodging in the villages along the route - comfortable, but rustic rather than luxurious, which is part of the appeal for a lot of travelers.

Three days is just enough to genuinely disconnect in the Atlas and come back with a real sense of accomplishment, not just a photo from a viewpoint. If that's the trip you're after, this is the itinerary that delivers it.
