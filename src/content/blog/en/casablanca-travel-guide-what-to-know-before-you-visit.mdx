Let's set expectations correctly, right now: Casablanca has almost nothing to do with the 1942 movie. It wasn't filmed there (it was filmed on a Warner Bros. lot in California), there's no Rick's Cafe waiting with Humphrey Bogart in it, and if you show up expecting sultry black-and-white nostalgia around every corner, you're going to be a little confused by the traffic and the office towers. What you actually get is Morocco's largest city and its economic engine - a big, modern, occasionally chaotic port city that most visitors fly into and fly out of within a day, which is honestly a bit of a shame, because it has a few things absolutely worth sticking around for.

Here's what you actually need to know.

## Getting to Casablanca

Mohammed V International Airport (CMN) is Morocco's main international gateway, so there's a decent chance you're landing here whether Casablanca was ever on your list or not. The airport has a direct train (the "Al Bidaoui" line) into Casa Voyageurs station in about 35 minutes, which is dramatically less stressful than fighting over a taxi fare after a long flight.

If Casablanca is just a layover before you head to Marrakech, Fes, or Rabat, the train network genuinely makes this easy - Casa Voyageurs connects to most major cities without needing to go back through the airport.

## Best Time to Visit Casablanca

Being right on the Atlantic gives Casablanca a much milder climate than the interior cities. Summers don't roast you the way Marrakech or Fes can, and winters stay relatively mild too.

- **Spring and autumn:** Comfortable and pleasant, as with most of Morocco.
- **Summer:** Warm, humid coastal air rather than scorching desert heat - genuinely one of the more comfortable big cities in the country in July and August.
- **Winter:** Mild but can be rainy and grey, since it's an Atlantic port city, not a desert oasis. Pack a light jacket, not a parka.

## Where to Stay

Most visitors base themselves near the city center or along the Corniche (the beachfront strip in Ain Diab), depending on what you're after. The center puts you closer to the Old Medina, the main squares, and the train station; the Corniche is more resort-like, with beach clubs, restaurants, and a proper sea view - a nice way to unwind if you've just come off a dusty desert tour and want a pool for a day.

## Top Things to Do in Casablanca

**Hassan II Mosque** - The reason most people actually plan time in Casablanca. It's one of the largest mosques in the world, built partly over the Atlantic Ocean, with a retractable roof and a minaret over 200 meters tall. Crucially, it's also one of the very few mosques in Morocco that non-Muslims can tour inside, via a guided visit - do not skip this, it's genuinely spectacular and worth the ticket price on its own.

**The Corniche and Ain Diab** - Casablanca's answer to a beach promenade: restaurants, beach clubs, surfers, and a long ocean walk that makes the city feel a lot less like an office park and a lot more like a coastal capital.

**The Old Medina** - Small and far less overwhelming than Fes or Marrakech's medinas, which honestly makes it a nice, low-stakes warm-up if this is your first Moroccan medina experience.

**Rick's Cafe** - Yes, it exists, and yes, it's a deliberate recreation built by a former American diplomat, opened in 2004, styled after the movie set. It's not "authentic" in any historical sense, but it's a genuinely well-done piano bar and restaurant if you want the nostalgia experience anyway - just go in knowing it's a themed homage, not a time machine.

**Habous Quarter (New Medina)** - Built by the French in the 1930s to look like a "traditional" Moroccan medina, which makes it a strange but interesting mix of colonial planning and authentic craft shops, bakeries, and small restaurants.

**Mohammed V Square and the Art Deco downtown** - Casablanca has one of the best collections of Art Deco and Mauresque architecture anywhere in Africa, a legacy of French colonial-era urban planning. Worth an hour of just walking and looking up.

## What to Eat

Being a major port city, Casablanca does seafood exceptionally well:

- **Fresh grilled fish and seafood** - head toward the Corniche or the central fish market for the freshest options.
- **Sandwich Casablancais / Moroccan sandwiches** - a genuinely underrated street food scene of stuffed baguette sandwiches sold from small stands across the city.
- **The usual Moroccan staples** - tagine, couscous, and pastilla are all available, though Casablanca's food scene leans more international and modern than the traditional cities further inland.

## Practical Tips

- **It's a working city, not a museum.** Traffic, business towers, and a genuine metropolitan pace are normal here - don't judge all of Morocco by Casablanca, and don't judge Casablanca by expecting it to be Marrakech.
- **A day or two is usually enough**, unless you're here for business. Most travelers treat it as a gateway city or a one-to-two-night stop rather than a full destination.
- **The tram** is an easy, cheap way to get around the center without dealing with taxi negotiations.
- **Book the Hassan II Mosque tour time in advance** if you can - tour slots are limited and it's worth planning around rather than showing up and hoping.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Casablanca worth visiting, or should I skip straight to Marrakech and Fes?**
If you have limited time, Marrakech and Fes offer a denser "classic Morocco" experience. But if you're already flying through Casablanca, even one day for the Hassan II Mosque alone is worth the stop.

**Is there really a Rick's Cafe from the movie?**
No original one - the current Rick's Cafe is a modern recreation opened in 2004, built specifically to capture the movie's atmosphere for visitors. It's a fun evening out, just not a historical site.

**Is Casablanca dangerous?**
No more than any large city its size. Normal city precautions apply - keep an eye on belongings in crowded areas, and use official taxis or the tram rather than unmarked cars late at night.

Casablanca isn't the Morocco of postcards, and it doesn't try to be. Treat it as the country's modern, working face rather than its museum piece, give the Hassan II Mosque the time it deserves, and you'll leave with a more complete picture of Morocco than the medinas alone can give you.
